## 1. Definition (one line)
A trademark is a distinctive sign — a brand name, word, logo, slogan, label, or shape — that identifies and distinguishes your goods or services from everyone else's. Registration gives you exclusive rights to that mark across India.

## 2. Primary action
Start your trademark enquiry. (Pre-tag service = Trademark.)

## 3. Who needs it
- You've launched or are launching a brand, product, or business name.
- You have a logo or slogan you don't want competitors copying.
- You're raising investment or franchising and need your brand as a protected asset.
- You've received a legal notice about your brand name.

## 4. What's included
- Trademark search and class advice.
- Preparation and filing of the application (Form TM-A).
- Handling of the prosecution process including objection replies and hearing representation (scope depends on the package).
- Status tracking through to the registration certificate.

## 5. Key facts
- **Governing law:** Trade Marks Act, 1999 and Trade Marks Rules, 2017; administered by the Registrar of Trademarks (IP India).
- **Validity:** 10 years, renewable indefinitely in 10-year terms.
- **Typical timeline:** ~6–12 months if unopposed and unobjected; 18–24 months if objections or oppositions arise (government-dependent).
- **Cost driver:** number of classes. Government fee is ₹4,500 per class for individuals/startups/small businesses; ₹9,000 per class for larger businesses (e-filing). *Verify against live IP India schedule before publishing.*

## 6. Types & classes
**Mark types:** word mark, logo/device mark, combined mark, service mark, and specialised marks (collective, certification, shape, sound).

**Classes:** 45 in total — Classes 1–34 for goods, Classes 35–45 for services. Common ones: Class 9 (software, electronics), Class 25 (clothing), Class 35 (business/retail services), Class 41 (education). A business spanning multiple categories needs each relevant class covered. Once TM-A is filed you may use the ™ symbol; the ® symbol is reserved for registered marks only.

## 7. The process (full prosecution chain)
1. **Trademark search** — check the IP India database for identical/similar existing or pending marks to avoid future conflict.
2. **File application (Form TM-A)** — submit online, select class(es), pay fees; an application number is generated immediately and ™ can be used.
3. **Vienna codification & formality check** — Registry classifies device marks and checks formalities.
4. **Examination** — examined under Section 9 (absolute grounds) and Section 11 (relative grounds); an Examination Report is issued.
5. **Objection — reply to Examination Report** — **deadline: within 1 month (30 days) of receiving the report**; extensions of up to two further months possible. No reply risks the application being treated as abandoned. *(An "objection" is raised by the examiner about the application itself — distinct from an "opposition," which is a third party challenge.)*
6. **Show cause hearing** — if the reply doesn't satisfy the examiner, a hearing is scheduled (usually by video conference). You demonstrate distinctiveness, often via evidence of actual use (invoices, ads, packaging). Adjournment via Form TM-M (fee ₹900), filed at least 2 days before, typically max two adjournments. Non-attendance can mean refusal or abandonment.
7. **Acceptance & advertisement** — accepted marks are published in the Trade Marks Journal.
8. **Opposition window** — **4 months** from advertisement, any person may oppose via Form TM-O. If opposed: opponent files notice → applicant files **counter-statement within 2 months (no extension — failure = deemed abandoned)** → evidence stages → hearing. Opposition is the longest branch, often 2–3 years.
9. **Registration & certificate** — if unopposed/resolved, the mark is registered and the certificate issued (Form RG-2). The ® symbol may now be used.

## 8. Documents required
- Copy of the trademark/logo (not needed for a plain word mark).
- Applicant name, address, nationality.
- Incorporation certificate (company/LLP).
- Udyog Aadhaar/MSME certificate (to claim the lower fee).
- Description of goods/services and the class.
- Power of Attorney (Form 48) signed by the applicant.
- PAN, Aadhaar, GST, partnership deed may also apply depending on applicant type.

## 9. Timeline detail
Unopposed and unobjected: certificate typically in 6–12 months. With objections/oppositions: 18–24 months owing to Registry backlog. Government stages are outside anyone's control — no registration outcome is guaranteed.

## 10. Cost drivers
Primary driver is the number of classes filed. Larger entities pay a higher per-class government fee. Objection replies, hearings, and opposition defence add scope. Link to Pricing.
