# Page Content — Patent Registration (`/services/patent`)

Uses the shared service-page template.

## 1. Definition
A patent grants you exclusive rights over an invention — a new product, process, or service — so others cannot make, use, sell, or import it without your permission.

## 2. Primary action
Start your patent enquiry. (Pre-tag service = Patent.)

## 3. Who needs it
- You've created a genuinely new invention, product, or process.
- You're a deep-tech or R&D-driven startup protecting core technology.
- You want to secure a priority date while you keep developing.
- You're planning to license or commercialise an invention.

## 4. What's included
- Patentability/prior-art search.
- Drafting of the specification (provisional or complete).
- Filing and prosecution support through examination to grant (scope depends on package).
- Guidance on Indian, convention, and PCT routes.

## 5. Key facts
- **Governing law:** Indian Patent Act, 1970; administered by the Controller General of Patents.
- **Patentability:** must be novel, involve an inventive step, and have industrial applicability.
- **Term:** 20 years from the date of filing; then it enters the public domain.
- **Typical timeline:** ~3–5 years end to end (government-dependent).

## 6. Types of application
- **Provisional** — temporary protection while the invention is still developing; secures a priority date.
- **Complete/Ordinary** — full protection within India; filed directly or after a provisional.
- **PCT National Phase** — entry into India from an international PCT application.
- **PCT International** — the international application stage.
- **Convention** — filed in India claiming priority from an earlier foreign filing (Paris Convention).
- **Divisional / Patent of Addition** — for multiple inventions in one application, or improvements to an existing patent.

**Provisional vs complete specification:** if the specification has no claims it's *provisional*; with claims it's *complete*. A provisional secures the priority date, and you then have **12 months to file the complete specification** (no extension). Provisional cannot be used for divisional, convention, or PCT national-phase applications — those require a complete specification.

## 7. The process
1. **Patent search** — search public databases to confirm novelty and non-obviousness and to see what's already registered.
2. **Draft the specification** — a technical + legal document; drafted by the inventor or a patent attorney.
3. **File** — Form-1 with the specification in Form-2 (provisional or complete).
4. **Publication** — application published in the Patent Journal at 18 months from the priority date.
5. **Request for Examination** — filed within the statutory period; examination reports and objections must be answered.
6. **Grant & publication** — if satisfied, the Controller grants the patent, subject to annual renewal fees.

## 8. Documents required
- Form-1 (application).
- Form-2 (complete or provisional specification).
- Form-3 (statement and undertaking).
- Form-5 (declaration of inventorship).
- Proof of the inventor's right to file.
- Form-26 (power of authority) if a patent agent files.
- For convention/PCT: priority documents within 18 months of the priority date.

## 9. Timeline detail
The full process typically takes 3–5 years. Key milestones: filing → provisional-to-complete within 12 months (if provisional) → publication at 18 months → examination on request → grant. Government timelines are outside anyone's control.
